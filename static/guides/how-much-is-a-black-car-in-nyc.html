How Much Is a Black Car in NYC?

Last updated: 20 November 2026

The honest answer: it depends on your trip. A black car fare is not one number — it depends on how far you are going, which class you choose, whether your route crosses a tolled bridge or tunnel, and how long your chauffeur waits for you. This guide explains each factor plainly, and how the exact total is shown to you before you pay.

The short answer

A short ride across Manhattan costs less than a long ride to the Hamptons, and a luxury sedan costs less than a First Class car — that much is obvious. What is less obvious is how the total is put together, and what is included in it. With Noel Chauffer, the exact total for your trip is shown to you at booking, before you pay — you see one single total, and you pay by card before a chauffeur is assigned. There are no surprises after the ride: after the ride ends, your invoice is sent to your phone and email.

Distance: the biggest factor

Distance is the biggest factor in any black car fare. A ride from LaGuardia to Midtown Manhattan is about 9 miles and usually takes around 25 to 40 minutes. A ride from JFK to Times Square is about 18 miles, around 40 to 60 minutes. A ride from Newark to Midtown Manhattan is about 16 miles, around 35 to 50 minutes. A ride from Valley Stream to JFK is about 7 miles, around 15 to 25 minutes. Longer rides take more of your chauffeur's time and more fuel, so the total is higher — and a ride to the Hamptons, Connecticut or upstate costs more than a ride across town, because the distance is far greater.

Traffic matters too, because it changes how long the ride takes. A 40-minute drive at midday can take over an hour at rush hour, and your chauffeur takes the best route for the time of day. The total shown at booking is the total for your trip as booked — it is not re-calculated because traffic was heavy.

Class: Business Class, Business SUV or First Class

The class you choose is the second biggest factor. Business Class is a luxury sedan, for up to 3 passengers with luggage. Business SUV is a luxury SUV, for more space and luggage. First Class is a Mercedes-Benz S-Class, BMW 7 Series, Audi A8, Tesla Model X, Bentley Bentayga, Bentley Flying Spur, Bentley Mulsanne, Mercedes-Benz Maybach S-Class, Rolls-Royce Cullinan, Rolls-Royce Ghost, Rolls-Royce Phantom or Lamborghini Urus, or similar — the grandest cars in the fleet, with the most space and comfort. You choose a class, and the exact model is never guaranteed — it is the class, or similar, that is guaranteed.

A larger, more luxurious car costs more than a sedan, because the car itself costs more to own and run. If you are travelling alone with a small bag, Business Class is the natural choice. If you have a family, a lot of luggage, or you simply want the most comfortable ride, Business SUV or First Class fits better — and the total reflects that.

Tolls on your route

Some routes cross tolled bridges or tunnels, and the toll is part of your total. Trips between New York and New Jersey cross the Hudson, and trips across the East River or into Manhattan can cross tolled crossings too. Trips that stay within one borough, or that use a free crossing, have no toll to add. Your chauffeur takes the best route for the time of day, and the toll for that route is included in the total shown at booking — you do not pay tolls separately at a booth.

Waiting time

Waiting time is included up to a point, and charged after it. Airport pickups include 60 minutes of free waiting — that covers most delays, slow baggage lines and long walks through the terminal. All other pickups include 15 minutes of free waiting. After the free waiting time, waiting charges apply. If you share your flight number when you book, your chauffeur follows your flight and adjusts to your new arrival time, so a delayed landing rarely eats into your free waiting time at all.

For hourly hire, the shape is different: you book a number of hours, and your chauffeur stays with you for those hours, with as many stops as your schedule needs. Your invoice after the ride reflects the hours you used.

Point to point vs by the hour

Point-to-point rides are one-way rides from one address to another, and the total is for that one ride. Hourly hire is for days with several stops: you book a number of hours, and the same chauffeur and car stay with you. Which one costs less depends on your day — one ride is cheaper point to point, while a day of meetings across Manhattan is usually simpler and better value by the hour, because you are not booking a new car at every stop.

What to check before you book

Before you book any black car, check four things. First, the total: is the exact total shown before you pay, or only estimated? With Noel Chauffer, you see one single total at booking and pay by card before a chauffeur is assigned. Second, the class: do you know which car you are getting — a sedan, an SUV, or First Class? Third, the waiting rule: how much free waiting do you get at the airport, and what happens after it? Fourth, the cancellation rule: with Noel Chauffer, cancellation is free more than 1 hour before pickup, with a full refund, and if you cancel 1 hour or less before pickup, the cancellation is not refundable. If you do not show up, the full fare applies.
