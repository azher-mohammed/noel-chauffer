JFK vs LaGuardia vs Newark

Last updated: 20 November 2026

New York has three major airports — JFK, LaGuardia and Newark — and the right one depends on where you are going, where you are flying from, and how much time you have. This guide compares all three on the things that matter for your ride into the city: distance to Manhattan, terminals, and which trips each airport fits best.

The short answer

If you want the shortest ride into Manhattan, fly into LaGuardia — it is about 9 miles from Midtown Manhattan, and the drive usually takes around 25 to 40 minutes depending on traffic. If you are flying internationally or long-haul, you will most likely land at JFK, which is about 18 miles from Times Square, around 40 to 60 minutes. If you are heading to New Jersey, or your flight uses Newark, fly into Newark Airport — about 16 miles from Midtown Manhattan, around 35 to 50 minutes depending on traffic and the river crossing.

There is no single best airport for everyone. The best airport is the one that fits your flight and your destination — and whichever one you choose, the pickup works the same way: your chauffeur meets you at the right terminal, follows your flight, and waits for you with 60 minutes of free waiting.

JFK — John F. Kennedy International Airport

JFK is New York's main international airport, in Queens. It has five terminals — 1, 4, 5, 7 and 8 — and handles most of the city's international and long-haul flights, along with plenty of domestic ones. JetBlue flies from Terminal 5, American Airlines from Terminal 8, and Delta's international flights use Terminal 4. Terminal 1 serves international carriers, including Air France, Lufthansa, Korean Air and others, and Terminal 7 serves British Airways, Alaska Airlines and others. The free AirTrain JFK connects all terminals, so moving between them is easy.

JFK is about 18 miles from Times Square, and the drive usually takes around 40 to 60 minutes depending on traffic. It is the furthest of the three airports from Manhattan, but it is also the one with the most flight options — especially if you are flying in from outside the United States. If your flight is international, JFK is very likely where you will land, and your chauffeur will meet you in the arrivals hall of your terminal with a name sign.

LaGuardia — the closest airport to Manhattan

LaGuardia (LGA) is the closest of the three New York airports to Manhattan — about 9 miles from Midtown Manhattan, and the drive usually takes around 25 to 40 minutes depending on traffic. It mostly serves domestic flights, along with flights to Canada and the Caribbean. It has three terminals — A, B and C. Terminal B serves Air Canada, American, Frontier, JetBlue, Porter, Southwest and United. Terminal C is Delta Air Lines and Delta Connection. Terminal A is the historic Marine Air Terminal. A free shuttle bus runs between the terminals landside.

Choose LaGuardia if you are flying domestically and your destination is Manhattan, Brooklyn or Queens. The shorter drive is the main advantage — you are in the city sooner, and the ride is simpler. The trade-off is fewer international flights: if you are flying in from outside North America, you will usually land at JFK instead.

Newark — Newark Liberty International Airport

Newark Airport (EWR) is in New Jersey, about 16 miles from Midtown Manhattan, and the drive usually takes around 35 to 50 minutes depending on traffic and the river crossing. It has three terminals — A, B and C. Terminal A is the new terminal, Terminal B serves international flights and other carriers, and Terminal C is United Airlines' hub. The AirTrain Newark connects all three terminals with the airport's train station.

Choose Newark if your destination is in New Jersey — Jersey City, Hoboken, Newark, Montclair, Princeton and beyond — or if your flight uses Newark. It also serves plenty of international flights, and for travellers heading to the west side of Manhattan it can be just as convenient as JFK. The river crossing is the one thing to plan around: at rush hour, the crossing adds time to the drive.

Which airport should you choose?

Here is the simple way to decide. Flying internationally or long-haul? You will most likely land at JFK, and that is fine — your chauffeur meets you at the right terminal and the drive to Manhattan is around 40 to 60 minutes. Flying domestically to Manhattan, Brooklyn or Queens? LaGuardia gives you the shortest ride into the city. Heading to New Jersey, or flying United? Newark is your airport. Travelling onward to Long Island or Westchester? JFK and LaGuardia are both convenient, and your chauffeur can take you to either from any of the three airports.

One more thing to check before you book your flight: your arrival time. All three airports are busiest at rush hour, and the drive into Manhattan takes longer then. An early-morning or late-evening arrival usually means a faster ride, whichever airport you land at. Your chauffeur takes the best route for the time of day.

Getting to Manhattan from each airport

From JFK, the drive to Times Square is about 18 miles and usually takes around 40 to 60 minutes. From LaGuardia, the drive to Midtown Manhattan is about 9 miles and usually takes around 25 to 40 minutes. From Newark, the drive to Midtown Manhattan is about 16 miles and usually takes around 35 to 50 minutes, depending on traffic and the river crossing. These are usual times — traffic, weather and road works can change them, and your chauffeur plans around the conditions on the day.
